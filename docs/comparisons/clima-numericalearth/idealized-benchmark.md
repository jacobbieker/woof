# woof vs ClimaAtmos.jl: idealized benchmarks

Status: the small end-to-end runs were done on a shared card. The
clean-card throughput runs are scripted in
`tools/compare/climaatmos_idealized/run_timing.sh` and have not been run
yet.

How the numbers below are labelled:

- **measured** — produced by this harness on this host.
- **documented** — taken from a cited source.
- **estimated** — derived by arithmetic from the other two.

## Why only idealized cases

ClimaAtmos.jl (<https://github.com/CliMA/ClimaAtmos.jl>) is a global
atmosphere model. It discretises the horizontal with spectral elements and
the vertical with finite differences, uses a HEVI IMEX ARS343 time stepper,
and is written in Julia with CUDA support through ClimaComms. Float32 is its
default precision (`config/default_configs/default_config.yml`,
`FLOAT_TYPE`).

It has no real-data limited-area mode. Its Cartesian `plane` and `box`
grids are periodic. The only like-for-like comparisons with woof's regional
model are therefore its own idealized configurations, run with the same
domain, resolution, terrain and precision in both models. This page covers
two of them:

- **Schär mountain wave**: `plane_schar_mountain_float32_test.yml`.
- **Dry density current**: `plane_density_current_test.yml`, which is
  Straka-like but not the Straka setup, see below. It is run once with
  ClimaAtmos's stock initial condition and once with the Straka et al.
  (1993) initial condition.

The RCEMIP-II box with one-moment microphysics and RRTMGP radiation
(`rcemipii_box_CRM_1M.yml`; RRTMGP.jl implements RRTMGP of Pincus et al.
2019) was **not run**. It would compare physics throughput with
non-identical physics, and the shared card did not allow a run of that
length.

## Setups

Hyperdiffusion on | same base state, terrain and sponge (`damp_opt=3`, `zdamp` 8 km, `dampcoef` 0.1). No explicit diffusion. Starts from u = U with w = 0 above the kinematic surface w |

Density-current initial conditions:

| IC | definition |
|---|---|
| ClimaAtmos stock (`src/setups/DryDensityCurrentProfile.jl`) | θ′ = −15 K·(1 + cos πr)/2, centred at z = 2 km, radii 4 km x 2 km |
| Straka et al. (1993) | T′ = −15 K at z = 3 km, so θ′ = T′/π_b |

`ca_driver.jl --straka-ic` redefines `DryDensityCurrentProfile`'s centre and
amplitude to Straka's. The rest of ClimaAtmos's profile code is unchanged.
The woof driver has `--ic clima` and `--ic straka`.

### Yardsticks

**Schär.** Both models' w is scored against ClimaAtmos's linear steady-state
solution (`src/topography/steady_state_solutions.jl`). It is a compressible
linear solution with a radiation condition, evaluated in η = (z − h)/(1 −
h/z_top). `analysis.schar_steady_state` ports it to NumPy and evaluates it
on each model's own w points. The port agrees with ClimaAtmos's own
`wapredicted` diagnostic to a relative L2 of 9.7e-4 (**measured**; the gap
is Float32 against float64). Scores cover all interior w points below the
13 km sponge base.

**Straka.** The converged reference (Straka et al. 1993, dx = 25 m, as
tabulated in later intercomparisons, e.g. Giraldo & Restelli 2008, JCP 227)
is θ′_min ≈ −9.77 K and a surface −1 K front at ≈ 15.54 km at t = 900 s.
These are **documented** values.

## Results: small end-to-end runs (measured, shared card)

Host: an NVIDIA RTX A6000 shared with a running woof forecast (about 33 GB
of 48 GB in use by other processes) and other benchmark workers. Every run
took the GPU lock `/tmp/woof-gpu-test.lock`, and each model was capped at
6 GiB (`JULIA_CUDA_HARD_MEMORY_LIMIT`, `CUPY_GPU_MEMORY_LIMIT`).

**Treat the absolute timings as indicative only.** Other processes'
kernels were sharing the SMs, and ClimaAtmos's own repeat measurements
varied by about 20%: 30.8 against 37.1 ms/step for two identical-cost
density-current runs.

Throughput is measured over 200 steps after a 20-step warm-up. The warm-up
covers Julia JIT and CUDA kernel compilation, and CuPy kernel loading. The
200 steps are bracketed by device synchronisation. In these accuracy-mode
runs the configs' output callbacks stay registered, but no output event
falls inside the timed block (hourly and 900 s periods).

Points counts unique degrees of freedom. ClimaAtmos also stores the
duplicated element-edge GLL nodes, which is 4/3 more stored points.

| case (grid) | model | dt (s) | ms/step | Mpoint-steps/s | simulated s per wall s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Schär 300 x 100 (30 000 pts) | ClimaAtmos | 0.7 | 30.9 | 0.97 | 22.7 |
| Schär 300 x 100 | woof | 2.0 | 7.95 | 3.77 | 251 |
| DC 240 x 90 (21 600 pts), stock IC | ClimaAtmos | 0.3 | 37.1 | 0.58 | 8.1 |
| DC 240 x 90, Straka IC | ClimaAtmos | 0.3 | 30.8 | 0.70 | 9.8 |
| DC 240 x 90, Straka IC | woof | 1.0 | 10.6 | 2.05 | 94.8 |
| DC 240 x 90, ClimaAtmos IC | woof | 1.0 | 10.3 | 2.09 | 96.8 |
| DC 512 x 64 (100 m, 32 768 pts), Straka IC | woof | 0.5 | 9.6 | 3.40 | 51.9 |

The woof Schär runs repeated under the same conditions took 7.9 to 9.1
ms/step. That spread is the shared-card noise.

At these sizes woof takes about 3.9 times less time per step on the Schär
grid and about 2.9 times less on the density-current grid. Because it can
also take a larger stable step (2 s against 0.7 s, and 1 s against 0.3 s),
it covers 10 to 11 times more simulated time per wall second (**measured**,
shared card).

Both grids are small: 2-D, with 2 to 3 x 10⁴ points. Both models are
therefore probably bound by kernel-launch and latency costs, not by memory
bandwidth. The ratio at 3-D sizes, where per-point efficiency dominates, is
exactly what `run_timing.sh` measures: a 135 x 135 x 45 and a
270 x 270 x 45 box, plus a 4x-wider Schär channel.

Start-up cost (**measured**, the same runs):

| model | start-up (s) | what it covers |
|---|---|---|
| ClimaAtmos, first config in a session | about 900 (592 setup + 304 warm-up) | build + compile |
| ClimaAtmos, second model type | about 410 | build + compile |
| ClimaAtmos, already compiled | about 15 | build |
| woof | under 1 | CuPy kernels cached on disk |

For short idealized runs, ClimaAtmos's start-up dominates the wall time.
The full 2 h Schär run took 1248 s of wall time in ClimaAtmos and 31 s in
woof.

### Accuracy

Schär mountain wave. Error of w against the linear solution below 13 km
(**measured**):

| run | t | rel. L2 error | correlation | RMSE (m/s) | max abs error (m/s) | max abs w, analytic / model (m/s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ClimaAtmos (u = U, w = 0 start) | 2 h | 0.102 | 0.995 | 0.0018 | 0.016 | 0.196 / 0.201 |
| woof, w = 0 above the kinematic surface w (the harness default, matching ClimaAtmos's start) | 2 h | 0.140 | 0.990 | 0.0025 | 0.020 | 0.193 / 0.184 |
| woof, the same | 6 h | 0.109 | 0.994 | 0.0020 | 0.016 | 0.193 / 0.185 |
| woof, WRF `fill_w` start w(0)·znw² (as `woof/verify/cases/hill2d.py`) | 2 h | 0.266 | 0.965 | 0.0048 | 0.063 | 0.193 / 0.184 |
| woof, the same | 6 h | 0.188 | 0.982 | 0.0034 | 0.026 | 0.193 / 0.185 |

ClimaAtmos's error against its own `wapredicted` is the same 0.102 to the
digits shown. Run names: `e2e_ca_schar`, `exp_woof_schar_surface_2h`,
`exp_woof_schar_surface_6h`, `e2e_woof_schar` and `exp_woof_schar_6h`.
`e2e_woof_schar` ran before the harness default changed, so it used the
WRF fill start. `run_e2e_small.sh` now writes that variant as
`e2e_woof_schar_wrffill`.

At matched start and time (2 h), ClimaAtmos is somewhat closer to the
linear solution: rel. L2 0.10 against 0.14. woof reaches ClimaAtmos's 2 h
level by 6 h.

How woof starts matters more than that gap. WRF's `fill_w` initial w
profile launches a start-up gravity-wave transient. At 2 h its largest
error is spurious upstream w aloft: 0.062 m/s at x = −27 km, z = 10.9 km,
where the analytic value is about 0. Its correlation falls from at least
0.999 below 1 km to 0.63 at 9 to 13 km. Starting from w = 0 above the
surface, as ClimaAtmos does, removes most of it.

ClimaAtmos's own regression test runs 24 h, and so does `run_timing.sh
ACCURACY=1`. The 24 h comparison of both models is still owed to the
clean-card phase.

Density current at t = 900 s (**measured**). The front is the surface −1 K
crossing, measured from the bubble centre:

| run | θ′_min (K) | front (km) | vs Straka (θ′_min, front) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Straka et al. 1993, 25 m (documented) | −9.77 | 15.54 | — |
| woof, Straka IC, 213 x 71 m, K = 75 | −10.6 | 15.38 | −0.83 K, −0.16 km |
| woof, Straka IC, 100 m, K = 75 | −9.55 | 15.38 | +0.22 K, −0.16 km |
| ClimaAtmos, Straka IC, 213 x 71 m, hyperdiffusion | −13.0 | 15.95 | −3.19 K, +0.41 km |
| ClimaAtmos, stock IC (bubble at 2 km), hyperdiffusion | −13.7 | 16.71 | n/a (no reference) |
| woof, ClimaAtmos stock IC, K = 75 | −10.7 | 15.87 | n/a (ClimaAtmos with the same IC: −13.7 K, 16.71 km) |

Both models keep the IC's mirror symmetry: the left and right fronts agree
to under 1 m.

The ClimaAtmos θ′_min is about 3 K colder than the reference. This is the
expected effect of running without Straka's K = 75 viscosity, which
ClimaAtmos cannot configure for momentum, not a resolution effect. The
θ′_min comparison is therefore not like-for-like. The front position, which
is less sensitive to the closure, is within 0.4 km of the reference for
ClimaAtmos and 0.16 km for woof. On its gated 100 m grid, woof's θ′_min is
within 0.22 K of the reference.

With ClimaAtmos's stock IC there is no reference. Run on the same grid, the
two models differ by 0.84 km in front position and 3 K in θ′_min. That gap
is consistent with the different closures (hyperdiffusion against K = 75),
so it should not be read as an error in either model.

## Answers so far

**Faster?** On these small 2-D idealized grids, woof is about 3 to 4 times
faster per step and 10 to 11 times faster in simulated time per wall
second. It also starts in under a second, against 7 to 15 minutes of JIT
for ClimaAtmos. These are **measured** on a shared card and indicative
only. Whether the per-point gap holds at 3-D, bandwidth-bound sizes is
**not yet measured**; `run_timing.sh` covers it.

**More accurate?**

- **Schär at 2 h, matched start:** ClimaAtmos is modestly closer to the
  linear solution (rel. L2 0.10 against 0.14; correlation 0.995 against
  0.990). woof reaches the same error by 6 h. With WRF's `fill_w` start,
  woof's 2 h error is 0.27, because of a start-up transient, not the
  steady wave.
- **Straka:** woof, with the published viscosity, is closer to the
  reference. ClimaAtmos's gap mostly reflects that it cannot run the
  reference closure.

**High resolution more efficiently?** This is not answerable from these
runs. The 4x-wider Schär and 3-D box timings in `run_timing.sh` are the
intended evidence.

There is also a documented consideration for scale-out. ClimaAtmos
supports multi-GPU runs through ClimaComms/MPI. As relayed by this
comparison's coordinator from Yatunin et al. (2026, JAMES,
doi:10.1029/2025MS005014), it showed weak scaling above 92% on Derecho
A100s (103 km to 6 km, 1 to 256 GPUs) and 0.20 SYPD on 256 H100s. This page
does not measure multi-GPU scale-out for either model. The often-quoted ClimaAtmos figure of
"≈223 ms/step for the moist baroclinic wave on one A100" could not be
checked against the paper (publisher page inaccessible) and is not used
here.

The raw `result.json` files for this page are in
`/home/jacob/wf-scratch/idealized-benchmark/runs/e2e_*` and `exp_*`.
